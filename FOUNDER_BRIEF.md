# ThesisOS — Founder Brief

## The problem
Investors have more information than ever and less clarity than ever. Watchlists tell you price movement. News feeds tell you what happened. AI summaries condense documents. None of those systems reliably remember the exact reason *you* own a security and monitor whether new evidence strengthens or weakens that reason.

## The product
ThesisOS is a thesis-monitoring layer for long-term investors.

A user adds a ticker and writes:
- why they own it
- what must remain true
- what would break the thesis

ThesisOS monitors new primary-source evidence and returns one of four states:
**Stronger · Unchanged · Weaker · Broken**

## Beachhead
U.S. public equities, starting with SEC filings.

This keeps the first data source authoritative, inexpensive, structured, and legally cleaner than building around social-media sentiment or trade signals.

## Differentiation
The product does not ask "What happened to this stock?"
It asks:
**"Did the evidence change relative to what this investor believed before?"**

That requires persistent thesis memory, not one-shot summarization.

## Initial pricing hypothesis
- Free — 3 theses
- Investor — $12/month, 25 theses
- Club — $29/month, shared workspace for five investors

## North-star metric
**Material thesis events that users mark "useful."**

Not daily active users. Not screen time. A good version of ThesisOS should reduce how often users need to stare at markets.

## Expansion path
1. SEC filings
2. earnings transcripts
3. company press releases / IR
4. insider transactions
5. institutional ownership
6. custom KPI extraction
7. management-language drift
8. collaborative investment clubs
9. crypto/on-chain thesis monitoring

## One-line pitch
**ThesisOS remembers why you own an investment and watches for evidence that changes the answer.**


## Current product surface

The working prototype now has three first-party SEC layers: material filings, standardized XBRL fundamentals, and Form 4 insider filing activity. The next integrations should be earnings transcripts, market/valuation data, and thesis-filtered news.
